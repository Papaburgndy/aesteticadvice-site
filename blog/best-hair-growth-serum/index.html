Minoxidil vs. Finasteride vs. Peptide Serums: What's the Difference?

If you have spent any time researching hair loss, you have probably encountered three categories of treatment: minoxidil, finasteride, and peptide-based or botanical serums. They work in fundamentally different ways, and understanding the distinction will save you months of trial and error.

Minoxidil (Topical)

Minoxidil is a vasodilator that was originally developed as a blood pressure medication. When researchers noticed unusual hair growth in trial participants, the topical form was approved by the FDA for both male and female pattern hair loss. It works by widening the blood vessels around hair follicles, prolonging the anagen (growth) phase of the hair cycle, and improving follicle thickness. It is available over the counter as a 2% or 5% solution or foam, and it requires daily use indefinitely to maintain results.

Finasteride (Oral, Prescription)

Finasteride is a 5-alpha reductase inhibitor that blocks the conversion of testosterone to dihydrotestosterone (DHT), the hormone responsible for the miniaturization of hair follicles in androgenetic alopecia. It is FDA approved for men and is among the most effective treatments for male pattern baldness, with clinical studies showing roughly 80 to 90 percent of users maintain or regrow hair over 12 months. Finasteride is not safe for women who are or may become pregnant due to risk of birth defects. It is prescription only and typically obtained through a doctor or telehealth provider. Many users combine finasteride with topical minoxidil for additive results.

Peptide and Botanical Serums

Peptide serums (like The Ordinary Multi-Peptide) use short chains of amino acids that signal hair follicles to extend the growth phase. Botanical serums (like Vegamour or Mielle) rely on plant extracts with mechanisms ranging from anti-androgen activity (saw palmetto) to vasodilation (rosemary oil). These serums are typically gentler, do not require prescription, and avoid the lifelong dependency of minoxidil. They are best used either as standalone treatments for mild thinning or as complements to clinical-strength options.

Which Should You Choose?

  • Visible thinning or pattern loss: Start with minoxidil 5% foam, and consider adding a peptide serum for layered support.
  • Diffuse shedding, postpartum, or stress-driven: Botanical or peptide serums are appropriate first-line options.
  • Male pattern baldness: Combine topical minoxidil with oral finasteride (consult a doctor) for the strongest evidence base.
  • Pregnancy or breastfeeding: Avoid minoxidil and finasteride. Most peptide and rosemary oil serums are considered safer, but check with your OB-GYN.

How to Use a Hair Growth Serum: Step-By-Step

Step-by-Step: Maximizing Results

1
Start with a clean, dry scalp

Apply your serum to clean, fully dry hair and scalp. Wet or damp hair dilutes the formula and reduces absorption.

2
Part your hair to expose the scalp

Section your hair so the serum lands directly on the scalp where follicles live, not on the hair strands.

3
Apply the recommended dose only

More product does not mean faster results. Stick to half a capful (for foams) or about 1 mL (for liquids). Excess product can cause irritation.

4
Massage gently for 30-60 seconds

A gentle scalp massage helps the serum absorb and may improve local blood flow. Do not rub aggressively.

5
Wash your hands and let it dry

Always wash your hands after application to avoid transferring active ingredients (especially minoxidil) to your face, eyes, or other unwanted areas.

6
Be consistent for at least 4 months

Skipping days or stopping early is the number one reason people see no results. Track your usage on a calendar or app, and reassess at month 4 with photos.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Quitting during the shedding phase. Minoxidil typically causes increased shedding in weeks 2 to 8 as old hairs are pushed out to make room for new growth. Stick with it.
  • Applying to wet hair. Wet hair dilutes the active ingredients and reduces efficacy. Always apply to fully dry hair.
  • Combining too many actives at once. Stacking three or four serums often causes scalp irritation and confuses the results. Start with one product and add others only if needed.
  • Not addressing nutrition. Iron, ferritin, vitamin D, and zinc deficiencies are common drivers of hair shedding. A serum will not work as well if these are out of range. Get bloodwork if shedding is sudden or severe.
  • Ignoring scalp health. A flaky, inflamed, or seborrheic scalp absorbs serums poorly. Address scalp conditions first with a salicylic acid or ketoconazole shampoo.

What to Pair With Your Serum

The right supporting routine amplifies your serum's results:

  • Scalp massager: Daily 2-5 minute scalp massage with a silicone brush has been shown to improve hair thickness over 24 weeks.
  • Biotin or marine collagen supplement: Modest evidence, but well tolerated for most people. See our notes on collagen supplements.
  • Low-level laser therapy (LLLT): Devices like laser caps and combs have a growing evidence base for androgenetic alopecia.
  • Sunscreen for the part: Your scalp burns. Sun damage can accelerate follicle miniaturization at the part line.

Frequently Asked Questions About Hair Growth Serums

Do hair growth serums actually work?

Some do, some do not. Serums with FDA-approved minoxidil are the most clinically validated for regrowing hair on the scalp. Peptide and caffeine serums can improve hair density and reduce shedding for many users, especially when used consistently for three to six months. Most pure-botanical serums have limited clinical evidence, though some users report improvements. The key is to set realistic expectations: serums slow loss and partially regrow hair but will not fully reverse advanced thinning.

What is the difference between minoxidil and finasteride?

Minoxidil is a topical vasodilator that increases blood flow to the hair follicles and prolongs the growth phase. It is FDA approved for both men and women and is available over the counter as a 2% or 5% solution or foam. Finasteride is an oral prescription medication that blocks the conversion of testosterone to DHT, the hormone primarily responsible for male pattern hair loss. Finasteride is FDA approved for men only and is not safe during pregnancy. Many men use both together for better results, while women typically rely on minoxidil and topical alternatives.

How long does it take to see results from a hair growth serum?

Most clinically tested serums require three to six months of consistent daily use before visible results appear. In the first four to eight weeks, you may notice increased shedding before regrowth begins. This is normal for minoxidil and indicates the product is working. By month three, reduced shedding and baby hairs at the hairline or part are common signs of progress. Stopping a serum will typically reverse any gains within three to six months.

Can I use hair growth serum with rosemary oil?

Yes, in most cases. Rosemary oil has been shown in some studies to perform comparably to 2% minoxidil for androgenetic alopecia. Many users alternate between rosemary oil and a clinical serum, applying one in the morning and the other at night, or use them on different days. If you experience scalp irritation, simplify your routine and reintroduce products one at a time.

Are hair growth serums safe during pregnancy?

Minoxidil is generally not recommended during pregnancy or breastfeeding. Finasteride is strictly contraindicated for anyone who is or may become pregnant due to risk of birth defects. Peptide and caffeine-based serums are typically considered safer, but you should consult your OB-GYN before introducing any new active topical product during pregnancy. Postpartum hair shedding is also common and often resolves on its own within six to twelve months.

Do I need to use minoxidil forever?

Yes, if you want to maintain your results. Minoxidil prolongs the growth phase of existing follicles, but does not permanently change your genetic hair loss pattern. Once you stop applying it, any regrowth you achieved will typically be lost within three to six months as the follicles return to their pre-treatment cycle. The same applies to most other clinical hair growth interventions, including finasteride.
